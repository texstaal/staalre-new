Negotiating a Large Warehouse Lease in the Netherlands: 10 Terms Worth More Than the Rent

By Tex Staal · Founder & Advisor, Staal Real Estate
TL;DR
  • The market has moved towards tenants: Savills put Dutch logistics vacancy at 6.5% in Q3 2026, against 2.6% in 2022. There is more room to negotiate, mainly on older buildings and secondary locations.
  • Landlords would rather give rent-free months than lower the headline rent, because the headline rent drives the value of their building. Compare offers on net effective rent.
  • On a large lease, the terms are worth more than a few euros of rent: the indexation cap, break and renewal options, expansion rights, power and the reinstatement standard.
  • Start 12 to 18 months before you need the space, and talk to more than one landlord at the same time.

Who is this guide for? Companies leasing roughly 5,000 m² or more in the Netherlands, where the lease is a commitment of millions. Take 10,000 m² at €75 per m² per year for ten years: that is €7.5 million in rent before indexation. At that size a better indexation cap or a break option can be worth more than a lower rent. For the clauses of the standard Dutch lease, see our guide to the ROZ lease; this article is about how to negotiate them.

Where the market stands in 2026

According to Savills, Dutch logistics vacancy was 6.5% at the end of Q3 2026, up from 5.7% a year earlier and 2.6% in 2022. Demand is recovering: take-up in Q3 2026 more than doubled to 767,847 m². Savills put the median logistics rent at €73 per m² per year and the prime rent at €115.

For a tenant that means more choice and more room on incentives and terms, especially for older stock and secondary locations. Modern buildings in the strongest locations still let quickly, so your leverage depends on the building. Rents by region are in warehouse rent prices in the Netherlands.

Headline rent versus net effective rent

Landlords prefer to keep the headline rent high and give incentives instead, because the headline rent sets the value of the building and the base for future indexation. So compare offers on net effective rent: the total rent over the term, minus incentives, spread over the years.

Headline rent€75 per m² per year€72 per m² per year

Rent over 10 years€7,500,000 − €375,000 = €7,125,000€7,200,000

Offer A looks more expensive but is cheaper over the term. Two catches: indexation is applied to the higher headline rent of offer A every year, and that headline is the starting point at renewal. And if you use a break option early, landlords often ask you to repay part of the incentive. Run the numbers with indexation and your own break dates before you choose.

The 10 terms that matter more than the rent

  1. Incentives. Rent-free months, a contribution to offices or fit-out, or landlord works such as extra dock doors, LED lighting or a sprinkler upgrade. Get each one in the heads of terms, with dates.
  2. Indexation. The ROZ model indexes the rent every year to the consumer price index. Ask for a cap, around 3–4% a year is a common request, and accept a floor if needed.
  3. Term, breaks and renewal. Warehouses are “230a” space under Dutch law: there is no statutory right to renew. Renewal and break options only exist if you negotiate them.
  4. Room to grow or shrink. A right of first refusal on adjacent units or land in the same park, or the right to hand back part of the space, can save a relocation later.
  5. Power and sustainability. Write the contracted grid capacity in kW into the lease, and agree who pays for an upgrade. Agree who owns and benefits from solar panels on the roof. See grid congestion and your warehouse.
  6. Service charges and measurement. Ask for the list of costs, a budget and an annual statement; for warehouses they typically run €5–15 per m² per year. Check that the floor area is measured to the Dutch standard NEN 2580, and that offices and mezzanines are priced separately.
  7. Security. A bank guarantee of three months’ rent plus service charges and VAT is common. A foreign group can offer a parent-company guarantee instead; ask for the amount to reduce after a period of on-time payment.
  8. Subletting and assignment. The right to sublet part of the space, and to transfer the lease to another company in your group without the landlord’s consent.
  9. Maintenance and installations. Who maintains the roof, sprinklers, dock levellers and heating? Ask the landlord to deliver everything in working order with valid inspection certificates.
  10. Reinstatement. Agree up front what you may leave behind at the end, such as racking anchors or a mezzanine, and record the condition at handover in a detailed report with photos.

How to run the negotiation

  1. Start 12 to 18 months ahead. Large fit-outs take time, and your current lease has a notice period. See our relocation timeline.
  2. Write one brief and ask several landlords for a proposal at the same time. Competition does more than any argument.
  3. Compare total occupancy cost: net effective rent, service charges, fit-out, reinstatement and the cost of any power upgrade.
  4. Agree heads of terms on paper: rent, term, incentives, options and security. They are usually non-binding, but they set the deal, and big landlords still need approval from their investment committee.
  5. Negotiate the lease itself with your lawyer, usually as special provisions in a ROZ lease.
  6. Check the building before you sign: zoning, permits, power and condition. Our due diligence guide lists what to ask.

Frequently asked questions

Can you negotiate warehouse rent in the Netherlands?

Yes. With Dutch logistics vacancy at 6.5% in Q3 2026 according to Savills, tenants have more room than in recent years, especially on older buildings and secondary locations. Landlords often move more on incentives and terms than on the headline rent.

What is a normal rent-free period for a warehouse lease?

There is no fixed standard. It depends on the market, the building, the location and above all the length of the lease: the longer you commit, the more a landlord will give. Compare offers on net effective rent rather than on the number of free months.

What is net effective rent?

The total rent over the lease term minus incentives such as rent-free months, spread over the years. For example: €75 per m² with six months free on a ten-year lease works out at €71.25 per m² per year.

How is warehouse rent indexed in the Netherlands?

Usually once a year, in the ROZ model to the consumer price index (CPI, all households) of Statistics Netherlands. Since 2025 parties may choose a different index if the lease names it. Many tenants negotiate a cap of around 3–4% a year.

How long is a typical warehouse lease in the Netherlands?

There is no statutory minimum for warehouses (“230a” space). Five years with a five-year renewal is a common structure, while new builds and large buildings often have longer terms. Because there is no right to renew, negotiate renewal and break options into the lease.
