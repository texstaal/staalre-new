Renting Cold and Frozen Warehouse Space in the Netherlands: Costs, Locations and What to Check (2026)

By Tex Staal · Founder & Advisor, Staal Real Estate
TL;DR
  • Decide the temperature first: chilled (0 to +8 °C), frozen (−18 to −25 °C) or deep-frozen (below −25 °C). It sets the building, the energy bill and who can supply it.
  • Small or seasonal volumes: rent pallet spaces from a cold-storage provider (indicatively €4–8 per pallet per week). Steady, large volumes: lease your own cold store.
  • The main clusters are the Rotterdam port, Dutch Fresh Port (Barendrecht–Ridderkerk), West Brabant, Venlo and Schiphol for pharma.
  • Before you sign, check the refrigerant (EU F-gas rules), grid capacity and who pays when the cooling fails. Need space? We run the search: lease warehouse space in the Netherlands.

Cold and frozen warehouse space is the scarcest, most technical corner of the Dutch warehouse market. The Netherlands is Europe’s cold-chain gateway: agricultural exports reached a record €137.5 billion in 2025, and the port of Rotterdam offers about 18,500 reefer connections, more than any other port in the world. Yet there are far fewer cold stores than ambient warehouses, most are run by specialist operators, and new ones are usually built for a single tenant on a long lease. This guide covers what you need to decide, what it costs, where to look, and what to check before you sign.

Chilled, frozen or deep-frozen: which do you need?

Start with the temperature, because it decides the building, the energy bill and how much space is available to you. A chilled store cannot be turned into a freezer by lowering the thermostat: freezer rooms need thicker insulation, a heated floor and a different refrigeration plant.

Many fresh-food operations need more than one zone: ripening rooms, a chilled dock and a frozen store under one roof. A cold store with several chambers that can switch between chilled and frozen is more flexible, and easier to sublet or hand back later.

As a rule of thumb, pay per pallet while volumes are small, seasonal or unproven, and lease once you run a steady, year-round operation with your own staff, processes or certification. Where the crossover lies depends on your volumes, how intensively you handle stock and how long you will stay, so model both before you choose.

What cold storage costs

Pallet storage

Pallet storage is quoted per pallet per week, plus handling in and out. Indicative Dutch quotes run to roughly:

  • Chilled (+2 to +8 °C): about €4–6 per pallet per week.
  • Frozen (−18 °C and below): about €5–8 per pallet per week.
  • Handling: about €1–3 per pallet in and out, plus admin and set-up fees.

These are indicative market quotes, not a benchmark. Rates vary with location, volume, contract length and extra services such as order picking, labelling or blast freezing. Always ask for an all-in rate per pallet including handling, because that is where quotes differ most.

Leasing a cold store

There is no public benchmark for Dutch cold-store rents: the market is small and most deals are private, often build-to-suit leases. What drives the rent is clear, though. A complete cold store costs roughly two to three times as much to build as a conventional warehouse, and the landlord prices that into the rent. For comparison, prime ambient warehouse rents run from about €55 to €125 per m² per year depending on the region (see our warehouse rent prices by region); expect a cold store to sit well above that range.

Energy is the other big number. Refrigeration runs around the clock, so budget energy as a major running cost alongside the rent, and make sure the lease sets out how it is metered and recharged.

Where the Dutch cold stores are

Cold storage clusters where fresh product enters the country, is grown, or is processed:

  • Rotterdam port. Europe’s main entry point for reefer cargo. Cold stores sit on the Maasvlakte and around the port’s fruit and juice terminals. Lineage bought Rotterdam-based Kloosterboer in 2022, adding 11 facilities and 790,000 pallet positions. See our Rotterdam guide.
  • Dutch Fresh Port (Barendrecht–Ridderkerk). The country’s largest fresh-produce cluster: around 100 fruit and vegetable companies with an export value of about €6 billion a year, next to the greenhouses of Greenport West-Holland. It is expanding onto the 96-hectare Nieuw Reijerwaard business park, where fruit and vegetable logistics specialist Kivits signed a 20-year lease on a new cold store.
  • West Brabant and Moerdijk. NewCold’s frozen warehouse in Dinteloord opened in April 2025: 134,000 pallet positions, 43 metres high, fully automated and running entirely off the public grid. See Breda–Moerdijk.
  • Venlo. Greenport Venlo combines horticulture, food processing and cross-border distribution into Germany. See Venlo.
  • Schiphol. Pharma and perishables by air. Pharma Gateway Amsterdam, a community of logistics companies, customs and the airport, runs a 7,200 m² airside facility with dedicated temperature-controlled areas, and many members are certified for GDP and IATA CEIV Pharma. See Schiphol / Amsterdam.

How these regions compare on labour, transport and cost is set out in choosing the right Dutch logistics hub.

What to check before you lease a cold store

A cold store is a machine as much as a building. These are the points that decide your running costs and your risk.

The refrigerant and the EU F-gas rules

Ask which refrigerant the plant runs on. Older systems often use R404A, a synthetic refrigerant with a very high climate impact. Since 2020, new R404A may no longer be used to top up larger systems, and under the revised EU F-gas Regulation (2024/573) newly produced gas of that kind can no longer be used for servicing at all, with recycled gas allowed only until 2030. From 2030, new stationary refrigeration systems may no longer use F-gases with a global warming potential above 150. An ageing system can therefore mean a forced, expensive replacement during your lease, so agree in writing that the landlord carries it.

Ammonia and CO₂ systems

Many large cold stores use natural refrigerants: ammonia or CO₂. Ammonia is efficient and has no climate impact, but in the Netherlands its use falls under the PGS 13 safety guideline, which affects permits, maintenance and what may be built nearby. Check that the permits cover the capacity and use you need.

Floor, panels and fire safety

  • Floor heating. Freezer floors need underfloor heating to stop the ground beneath from freezing and heaving. Ask about its condition and who maintains it.
  • Insulated panels. The sandwich panels decide both energy use and fire risk. Check the panel type, and that your insurer accepts it.
  • Sprinklers. Frozen areas need dry-pipe or pre-action sprinkler systems that cannot freeze. Check the fire strategy covers how you will use the building.

Grid capacity and energy

Refrigeration needs a lot of reliable power, and with grid congestion (netcongestie) across much of the country, extra capacity can take years to arrange. Check the contracted connection, whether it covers your peak load, and what backup power is in place. NewCold’s off-grid store in Dinteloord shows how far operators now go to secure their power.

Docks and the cold chain

Temperature is lost at the dock. Look for insulated dock doors with dock shelters, and a chilled loading area or ante-room in front of frozen chambers, so product never waits in warm air.

Lease terms that matter more in a cold store

  • Who maintains and replaces the refrigeration plant, and how quickly they must respond to a failure.
  • Liability for stock lost when the cooling fails, and how you insure it.
  • How energy is metered and recharged.
  • Reinstatement at the end of the lease: removing specialist installations can be expensive.

For how Dutch leases work in general, from the ROZ model to break options and indexation caps, see our guide to leasing warehouse space in the Netherlands.

The market: scarce, consolidating and built to order

Cold storage is a smaller, tighter market than ambient warehousing. CBRE describes much of Europe’s cold-storage stock as 25 to 30 years old, large operators are consolidating the market, and new cold stores are mostly developed for a named user on a long lease, as with the Kivits store in Ridderkerk. For a tenant, that means:

  • Start early. For a dedicated cold store, allow 12 to 24 months.
  • Consider build-to-suit if nothing existing fits; long leases are normal there, and so is negotiating the specification.
  • Look beyond the hotspots. Space away from the port, Barendrecht and the Westland is often easier to secure and priced more keenly.
  • Be realistic about conversions. Chilling an ambient warehouse can work; converting one to frozen means new insulation, a heated floor, new plant and more power, and rarely pays.

Frequently asked questions

How much does cold storage cost per pallet in the Netherlands?

Indicatively about €4–6 per pallet per week for chilled storage and €5–8 for frozen, plus roughly €1–3 per pallet for handling in and out. Rates vary with location, volume, contract length and services, so compare all-in quotes.

What temperature is frozen storage?

Frozen storage usually runs at −18 °C to −25 °C. Deep-frozen storage, for products such as ice cream and fish, runs below −25 °C, while chilled storage sits between 0 and +8 °C.

Can I rent a small amount of cold storage?

Yes. For small or seasonal volumes, rent pallet spaces from a cold-storage provider, or hire mobile cold cells for short peaks. Leasing your own cold store makes sense for steady, larger volumes; we arrange warehouse leases from 500 m².

Where is cold storage concentrated in the Netherlands?

Around the port of Rotterdam, in the Dutch Fresh Port cluster in Barendrecht–Ridderkerk, in West Brabant around Moerdijk, in Venlo, and at Schiphol for pharmaceuticals and air-freight perishables.

Can an ambient warehouse be converted into a cold store?

Converting to chilled use is often feasible. Converting to frozen means new insulated panels, a heated floor, a refrigeration plant and much more power, which rarely pays compared with leasing an existing cold store or a build-to-suit. Check permits and grid capacity first.
