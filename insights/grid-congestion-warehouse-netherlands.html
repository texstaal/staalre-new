Grid Congestion in the Netherlands: How to Secure Power for Your Warehouse (2026)

By Tex Staal · Founder & Advisor, Staal Real Estate
TL;DR
  • The Dutch electricity grid is full in many regions. On 30 June 2026, 15,498 requests for new or heavier offtake connections were waiting with the regional grid operators, according to Netbeheer Nederland.
  • For a warehouse, power is now a location criterion, like road access: automation, electric trucks, charging and cooling all need it.
  • Check the existing connection and its contracted capacity in kW before you shortlist a building, and write that figure into the lease or purchase contract.
  • If the building falls short, there are options: a flexible grid contract, a group contract with neighbours, solar with batteries, or a different building.

Who is this guide for? Companies that lease or buy warehouse space of roughly 2,000 m² or more in the Netherlands, and especially those that automate, electrify their trucks and vans, or store goods chilled or frozen. A few years ago the electricity connection was a footnote in a property search. Today it can decide whether a building works at all.

What grid congestion means

Grid congestion (netcongestie) means the cables and substations in an area are at their limit at peak times. Until the grid is reinforced, the grid operator cannot promise new transport capacity, so new requests join a waiting list. It affects both directions: offtake (using power, afname) and feed-in (delivering power to the grid, for example from a solar roof, teruglevering).

The regional grids are run by operators such as Liander, Stedin and Enexis; TenneT runs the national high-voltage grid. Which operator you deal with depends on the address.

How big is the problem in 2026?

Netbeheer Nederland’s half-year update, reported on 5 October 2026, gives the scale:

  • 15,498 requests for offtake were waiting with the regional grid operators on 30 June 2026, up from 15,154 at the end of 2025.
  • Together they asked for 9,986 MW of transport capacity.
  • Since 1 July 2026, smaller connections also join the waiting list, not only large users.
  • Part of the province of Utrecht has a full connection stop, and the regions around Amersfoort and the Food Valley are also closed for now; even priority projects there can only be connected from 2028.

The grid operators say the waiting list is growing more slowly, and in some regions has started to fall. But building new cables and substations is behind plan, so in the busiest areas the shortage will last for years.

Why it matters more for a warehouse than it used to

  • Automation: shuttle systems, sorters and automated storage draw far more power than a traditional pallet warehouse.
  • Electric transport: a few fast chargers for trucks or vans can need more power than the whole building used before.
  • Cooling and freezing: a cold store runs around the clock. See our guide to cold storage warehouses.
  • Heating without gas: heat pumps for offices and production areas move demand from gas to electricity.
  • Solar: where feed-in is congested, a solar roof may not be able to export its surplus. The power is then worth most when you use or store it on site.

Small or large connection: know which one you have

Connections up to 3×80 A count as small (kleinverbruik). Anything heavier is a large connection (grootverbruik), and for those two figures matter:

  • the size of the physical connection, and
  • the contracted transport capacity in kW (gecontracteerd transportvermogen): the maximum the building may draw under its contract with the grid operator.

The second figure is the one that counts. In a congested area, a building with a big cable but a small contract can be stuck at that contract for years.

How to check a building before you shortlist it

  1. Ask the owner for the connection details: the EAN code, the connection size, the contracted transport capacity and the peak demand of the last twelve months.
  2. Estimate your own peak demand, now and in five years: automation, chargers, cooling, offices. An installer or energy consultant can calculate it from your operation.
  3. Look up the area on Netbeheer Nederland’s capacity map, which shows where there is a waiting list for offtake and for feed-in.
  4. Ask the grid operator early if you need more: what is possible at this address, by when, and at what cost. Do this before you sign, not after.
  5. Put it in the contract: the contracted capacity in kW, that it stays available to you, and what happens if it does not. In a multi-tenant park, check how a shared connection is divided between tenants.

We add these questions to the wider building checks in our warehouse due diligence guide.

What to do when the power isn’t there

  • A flexible grid contract. Grid operators now offer alternative transport rights alongside the standard “firm” contract: for example a right that applies only in agreed time blocks, or a variable right that the operator may limit at busy moments. In exchange you pay a lower grid tariff. This suits operations that can shift charging or cooling to quieter hours.
  • A group contract on the business park. With a group transport agreement (groepstransportovereenkomst), several companies share one contracted capacity. It works when their peaks fall at different times, and it is the basis of most “energy hubs” on Dutch business parks.
  • Your own measures. Solar with batteries, peak shaving and smart charging can keep you under the contracted capacity. A large battery needs its own checks, including fire-safety rules for lithium-ion storage (PGS 37).
  • A different building. Sometimes the fastest answer is a building that already has a heavy connection. That is why a strong connection now adds real value to a property.

Don’t count on priority. Dutch rules allow grid operators to give priority to projects that relieve congestion or serve social needs such as safety, health care and education. Most warehouse projects do not qualify, so expect first come, first served.

What it means for rent, price and negotiation

A building with spare capacity lets and sells faster, and owners know it. When the connection needs an upgrade, who pays is negotiable: the connection belongs to the building, so a tenant can ask the landlord to pay for or contribute to it, sometimes against a slightly higher rent. Agree a fallback too, such as a break option if the extra capacity is not delivered by an agreed date. More on that in negotiating a large warehouse lease. Planning a new building? Then the connection request often takes longer than construction itself; see build-to-suit warehouses.

Frequently asked questions

What is grid congestion in the Netherlands?

It means the electricity grid in an area is at its limit at peak times, so the grid operator cannot promise new or extra transport capacity until the grid is reinforced. New requests join a waiting list. On 30 June 2026, 15,498 requests for offtake were waiting with the regional grid operators.

How long is the wait for a heavier grid connection?

There is no single answer: it depends on the address and the capacity you need. In the busiest areas it can take years, and parts of Utrecht, Amersfoort and the Food Valley are closed, with even priority projects only connected from 2028. Ask the regional grid operator for an indication for the specific address before you sign.

How do I check the grid capacity of a warehouse?

Ask the owner for the EAN code, the connection size, the contracted transport capacity in kW and the peak demand of the last twelve months. Compare that with your own expected peak, check the area on the Netbeheer Nederland capacity map, and write the contracted capacity into the lease or purchase contract.

Can solar panels solve a shortage of grid capacity?

Partly. Solar helps during the day, especially combined with a battery and smart charging. But feed-in is congested in many areas too, so the surplus may not be exportable. It works best when you use or store the power on site.

Who pays for a heavier connection when you lease a warehouse?

It is negotiable. The connection belongs to the building, so many tenants ask the landlord to pay for or contribute to the upgrade, sometimes against a slightly higher rent. Agree in the lease what happens if the extra capacity is not delivered on time.
