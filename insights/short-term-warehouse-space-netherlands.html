Short-Term Warehouse Space in the Netherlands: Your Options (2026)

By Tex Staal · Founder & Advisor, Staal Real Estate
TL;DR
  • Dutch law sets no minimum lease term for warehouses, so a short lease of one to three years is possible. Five years is the market habit, not a rule.
  • There are five realistic routes to short-term warehouse space: a short lease, a sublease, pallet storage with a 3PL, a temporary hall, or a longer lease with an early break.
  • Pallet storage costs roughly €2–6.50 per pallet per week before handling. A temporary 500 m² hall rents for about €1,500–4,500 a month for the structure alone.
  • With vacancy up to about 6.3%, more landlords will talk about shorter terms in 2026. Compare the routes in the options table or start a search.

Can you rent warehouse space in the Netherlands for a few months, or a year or two? Yes, but rarely through a standard lease. Most Dutch warehouse leases run five years or more, so short-term warehouse rental means choosing a different route: a short lease where a landlord has empty space, taking over part of another company’s warehouse, storing pallets with a logistics provider, or putting up a temporary hall. Each route has its own cost, speed and fine print. This guide compares them, so you can pick the one that fits your timeline.

Why Dutch warehouse leases usually run five years or more

Most Dutch warehouse leases are written on the ROZ model, and the market default is five years with a five-year extension. Landlords like long terms because they finance and value buildings on secured income, and the rent-free months and fit-out contributions they offer only pay back over a longer period.

The law itself is more flexible. Warehouses, distribution centres and other storage buildings fall under article 7:230a of the Dutch Civil Code, and for these premises there is no statutory minimum lease term. That is the difference with shops and restaurants, which have a minimum of five plus five years. You and a landlord can agree on six months, a year or three years.

What makes landlords careful is eviction protection. When a 7:230a lease ends, the tenant can ask the court to postpone the eviction, and that protection can last up to three years in total. A landlord who lets space for one year therefore wants certainty that you will actually leave. Expect clauses that make the end date firm, and read them with care if there is any chance you will need the space longer.

The market is on your side for now. Vacancy in Dutch warehouse space has risen to around 6.3%, and research firm BCI expects it to keep rising in Flevoland/Lelystad, Twente, Groningen, Arnhem–Nijmegen and South Limburg. A landlord with an empty unit and no tenant in sight is far more open to a short lease than one in a fully let park near Rotterdam.

1. A short lease directly from a landlord

The cleanest option if you run your own operation: your own unit, your own docks, your own staff. Short leases come up in multi-let business parks, in older buildings, in buildings waiting for redevelopment, and with any landlord who has had a unit empty for a while. Expect the rent at or above market level, few or no rent-free months, and the unit taken as it is. A one-year lease will not pay for a new floor or extra docks.

2. A sublease from another occupier

Companies that have outgrown their plans often sit on surplus space, and they would rather recover part of the rent than pay for empty floor. A sublease can therefore come in below market rent, sometimes with racking already in place. The catch: the standard ROZ conditions do not allow subletting without the landlord’s prior written consent, and your rights end when the main lease ends. Make sure that consent is in writing before you move in, and check the end date of the main lease.

3. Pallet storage with a logistics provider

If you only need to store goods, a third-party logistics provider (3PL) is the most flexible route. You pay per pallet per week, and the provider runs the warehouse, the staff and the systems. Indicative Dutch rates for ambient storage are €2.00–5.50 per pallet per week in block storage and €2.80–6.50 in racking, with the lowest rates for volumes above 500 pallets. Handling, order picking and transport come on top, so always ask for an all-in price.

A quick comparison shows where the line is. Storing 500 pallets in racking at €2.80–4.50 a week costs roughly €73,000–117,000 a year before handling. A 1,500 m² unit at €70/m² costs about €105,000 a year in rent, but then you still need your own racking, forklifts, staff and systems. Pallet storage usually wins until you need your own team on site anyway.

4. A temporary hall

If you control land (next to your current warehouse, or on a yard you lease), a temporary hall can add space fast. One Dutch supplier puts the rent for a 500 m² hall at €1,500–4,500 a month for the structure, with transport, assembly, flooring, power, lighting and heating adding another 30–50%. Building it takes days; preparing the site takes one to three weeks.

The permit is usually the slowest part. Many municipalities require an environment permit (omgevingsvergunning) for a temporary hall, and under the regular procedure the municipality has eight weeks to decide, extendable by six. Ask the municipality early, and check with your insurer, because a tent hall full of stock is a different fire risk from a sprinklered warehouse.

5. A longer lease with an early break

If you are not sure how long you need the space, a short lease is not always the cheapest answer. A five-year lease with a break option after two or three years often comes with rent-free months and a lower rent, which a short lease does not. Over the first two years, that can make the effective rent lower than a short lease, and you keep the option to stay. For how break options and indexation work in a Dutch lease, see our guide to leasing warehouse space in the Netherlands.

What to check before you sign a short-term deal

  • Permitted use. The omgevingsplan (zoning plan) must allow your activity at that address. If you store hazardous substances, the PGS 15 guideline sets extra requirements for the building.
  • The end date and any extension. If you might need the space longer, negotiate an extension option now. It is much harder to get once the landlord has lined up the next tenant.
  • Reinstatement. On a short lease, the cost of returning the unit in its original state weighs heavily. Agree a condition report with photos at the start, and limit what you have to undo at the end.
  • Deposit and guarantees. Landlords commonly ask for three months’ rent as a deposit or bank guarantee, even on a short lease. See what you need in place to lease.
  • Power. With grid congestion (netcongestie) across much of the country, a bigger connection can take years. On a short lease, you work with the power the building has, so check that it covers your charging, lighting and any cooling.
  • Fire safety and insurance. Check that sprinklers and fire compartments suit your goods, and that your insurer accepts the building before you move stock in.
  • Total cost. Service charges, energy and municipal taxes come on top of the rent, even for a short stay. See what that adds up to in our overview of warehouse rent prices in the Netherlands.

Where to find short-term space

Short leases and subleases rarely reach the property portals. A landlord willing to let for one year often prefers not to advertise it, and a company with surplus space usually tries its own network first. The supply also depends on the region: the eastern and southern regions have more vacancy than the Randstad and the Rotterdam port corridor, so a short lease near Venlo or Nijmegen is easier to find than one near Schiphol. Frequently asked questions

Can you rent a warehouse for a few months in the Netherlands?

Yes. Dutch law sets no minimum term for warehouse leases, so a lease of a few months is legally possible, but most landlords want at least one year. For a few months, pallet storage with a logistics provider or a sublease from another company is usually easier to arrange than a lease of your own.

Is there a minimum lease term for a warehouse in the Netherlands?

No. Warehouses fall under article 7:230a of the Dutch Civil Code, which has no statutory minimum term. The market standard is a ROZ lease of five years with a five-year extension, but shorter terms can be agreed, especially with landlords who have empty space.

How much does short-term warehouse storage cost in the Netherlands?

Pallet storage with a logistics provider costs roughly €2.00–5.50 per pallet per week in block storage and €2.80–6.50 in racking, before handling. A temporary 500 m² hall rents for about €1,500–4,500 a month for the structure, plus 30–50% for assembly, flooring and power. A short lease is usually at or above market rent, with few incentives.

Can I sublet part of a warehouse in the Netherlands?

Only with the landlord’s prior written consent. The standard ROZ lease conditions do not allow subletting without it. As the subtenant, also check the end date of the main lease, because your rights end when it ends.

How quickly can I get short-term warehouse space?

Pallet storage can start within days. A short lease or sublease typically takes two to six weeks to find and sign. A temporary hall takes one to three weeks to prepare, but if it needs an environment permit, the municipality has eight weeks to decide, extendable by six.
