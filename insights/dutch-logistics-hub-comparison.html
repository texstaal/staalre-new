Rotterdam, Venlo or Tilburg: Choosing the Right Dutch Logistics Hub

By Tex Staal · Founder & Advisor, Staal Real Estate
TL;DR
  • Rotterdam fits container-heavy flows, Venlo serves the German market, Tilburg–Waalwijk is the e-commerce middle ground, and Schiphol handles high-value air cargo.
  • Decide on your flows first, then price labour (not just rent) and check grid capacity (netcongestie) before you shortlist.
  • Compare every region on our Dutch logistics hubs map, see rents by region in warehouse rent prices in the Netherlands, or go straight to leasing warehouse space.

Rotterdam fits container-heavy flows, Venlo serves the German market, Tilburg–Waalwijk is the e-commerce middle ground, and Schiphol handles high-value air cargo. “Where should we be?” is usually an international tenant’s first question, and the honest answer is: it depends on what your network needs to do. The Netherlands is small, but its logistics geography is sharply differentiated, here is how the main regions compare.

Rotterdam & the port corridor

If your flows are container-heavy, proximity to the Port of Rotterdam compresses drayage cost and time. The Maasvlakte and surrounding zones (Botlek, Waalhaven, and the A15 corridor) offer deep-sea connectivity no inland location can match. The trade-offs: scarce land, premium rents, and a tight labour market shared with the port itself.

Venlo & the southeast

Venlo sits on the German border with tri-modal access (road, rail, barge) and ranked first in Prologis’s studies of Europe’s most desirable logistics locations in 2013, 2016 and 2017. It’s the natural choice for distribution into the German Ruhr and wider European market. Large-footprint, modern stock is more available here than in the Randstad, and labour, often supported by international workforce agencies, is comparatively accessible.

Tilburg–Waalwijk & central Brabant

The Tilburg region balances national coverage with European reach: barge terminals, strong XXL warehouse stock, and an established e-commerce fulfilment cluster. For a single-site Dutch operation serving both NL and cross-border e-commerce, central Brabant is frequently the rational compromise.

Schiphol & the Randstad

Air-cargo-dependent and high-value, low-volume flows (electronics, pharma, fashion) cluster around Schiphol and Amsterdam. Expect the country’s highest rents, smallest units, and most competitive leasing processes, but unmatched same-day reach into the Benelux’s wealthiest consumer base.

How to actually decide

  • Map your flows first. Inbound port vs. air vs. road; outbound B2B vs. B2C; where your customers actually are.
  • Price labour, not just rent. A €5/m² saving means little if you can’t staff a second shift.
  • Check power early. Grid congestion (netcongestie) is real; sites with secured capacity carry a premium for a reason.
  • Visit before you shortlist. Parks that look identical on paper differ enormously in access, neighbours and condition.

Frequently asked questions

Which Dutch logistics hub is best for my operation?

It depends on your flows: Rotterdam for container-heavy imports, Venlo for the German market, Tilburg–Waalwijk for national e-commerce fulfilment, and Schiphol for high-value air cargo. Our location guides break down each hub’s strengths, rents and stock.

Why did Venlo rank as Europe’s top logistics location?

Venlo sits on the German border with tri-modal access (road, rail and barge) and ample land. It topped Prologis’s European ranking on availability of land, road access, transport cost and regulation, which makes it the default for distribution into Germany.

What matters as much as rent when choosing a hub?

Labour and power. A low rent means little if you can’t staff a second shift, and grid congestion (netcongestie) can stall a site entirely. Price the labour catchment and confirm secured power capacity before you shortlist a single building.
